===========================================================================
Summary
===========================================================================

This guide explained how clinical and public health laboratories could validate bioinformatics pipelines within an ISO 15189 context. It began by describing why genomics is now embedded across clinical and public health practice, why bioinformatics pipelines are multi-component and complex, and why validating them is harder than validating a conventional wet laboratory method, given their sensitivity to software versions, parameters, reference datasets, and computational environment. It set out the principles of validation under ISO 15189, including fitness for intended use, defined performance characteristics, validation before clinical use, verification of externally validated methods, ongoing monitoring and revalidation, documentation and traceability, and a risk-based approach to deciding how much validation evidence is required. It argued that performance criteria should be defined before any code is written, derived from the clinical question the pipeline is intended to answer and the consequences of an incorrect result. It then explained how to develop a validation plan, covering the rationale for the chosen method, the validation dataset, what would be measured and how, minimum testing criteria, and a schedule of activities.
